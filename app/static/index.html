The melanoma warning

On its own, the model's top answer catches about half of melanomas. Missing a melanoma is far worse than an unnecessary check-up, so the site also warns whenever the chance of melanoma passes a small threshold.

Correct answers by lesion type

Why this model

It came out joint best of four models compared on the same data (a CNN from scratch, ResNet50, EfficientNet-B0 and MobileNetV3), caught the most melanomas on average across its training runs, and is small and fast enough for any laptop. Full study: Skin-Disease-Model-Comparison.

How it was trained

HAM10000, 10,015 dermoscopy photos of 7 lesion types, split by lesion so no lesion appears in both training and testing. ImageNet-pretrained EfficientNet-B0, fine-tuned for 30 epochs with rotation, flip, zoom and colour augmentation and a class-weighted loss.

Two test sets

2,004 held-out HAM10000 photos, and 1,511 photos from the separate ISIC 2018 test set, which checks the model copes with images from a different collection.

Limits

Trained on dermoscopy photos (taken through a skin magnifier), so ordinary phone photos will be less reliable. Only knows 7 lesion types. Not a medical device.

Your privacy

Skin photos are personal, so this app is built to forget them.

Photos are never saved

A photo is held in memory only while the model looks at it, well under a second, and is then discarded. It is never written to disk.

No photos in the database

The database holds only usernames, hashed passwords and login sessions. It has no place for images, and automated tests check that none get in.

Nothing leaves this computer

The model runs here. No cloud service, no API, no tracking. The website only accepts connections from this computer.
